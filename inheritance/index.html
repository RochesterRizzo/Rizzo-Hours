The Inheritance of Western Civilization

What was inherited. What socialism and communism destroyed. How Critical Theory, postmodernism, and identity ideology moved the assault into culture and institutions. What must be defended, recovered, transmitted, and built.

The purpose of this project

This is not a euphemism factory. Ideas have consequences, destructive ideas can migrate from books into institutions, and a civilization that cannot name or transmit its inheritance will eventually lose it.

We will preserve sharp arguments, read original texts, test claims against the historical record, trace mechanisms into institutions, distinguish demonstrated genealogy from asserted genealogy, and ask what ought to be built in place of endless dismantling.

What is true, good, beautiful, and worth transmitting—and who will have the courage to transmit it?

Read originals
Not slogans or descendants.
Follow mechanisms
Resemblance is not genealogy.
Count consequences
Archives, testimony, rules, outcomes.
End with construction
Preserve, repair, teach, build.

Map of the project

1. The inheritance

Reason, truth, law, dignity, constitutional government, property, markets, science, religion, family, duty, beauty, freedom.

2. Socialism and communism

Alienation, class, revolution, equality, planning—and the record of famine, terror, camps, surveillance, and conformity.

3. Critical Theory

Frankfurt: reason, mass culture, authority, family, tolerance, personality, repression, permanent critique.

4. Postmodernism

Power/knowledge, discourse, deconstruction, meaning, anti-foundationalism, subject formation, American reception.

5. Identity and institutions

Intersectionality, CRT, queer theory, privilege, equity, anti-racism, gender ideology; universities, schools, NGOs, HR, media, government.

6. Recovery and reconstruction

Truth, moral judgment, ordered liberty, pluralism, free inquiry, family, association, constitutional limits, science, beauty, courage, builders.
