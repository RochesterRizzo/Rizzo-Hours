Course timing and readings

We are going to start the course by exploring perceptions and doing triage on environmental problems, discussing risk and diving a tiny bit into the history of the environmental movement.

  • Please bring $5 to our first class (sometimes we don’t pay/collect until class two, but be prepared)
  • If you prefer, we can use Venmo (@Riz20).
  • Please make your way through the readings in Section One.
  • If you read anything for the first class, please read Laurence Tribe’s instrumental piece on Ways to Not Think About Plastic Trees (it’s also part of the first homework).

Class Format: In person. Note that I have asked the college to record the lectures with the in-class technology (not on Zoom) but this will not be available for general use.

Syllabus: Please read it through, carefully. Make note of all important dates on your calendars now; start organizing your projects now.

Assignments and due dates

There will be an assignment due every Sunday night. Each week’s work will include at least one research project, a few review/preview economics questions based on the class lectures, and an opportunity to respond to one or more academic and non-academic pieces. The project for the first week is a little shorter than you can expect for the rest of the semester. We strongly recommend starting early on each of the assignments.

  • Your first assignment has been posted and is due uploaded to Blackboard before 6:59pm on Sunday, August 31st.
  • Late assignments, regardless of the reason, will not be accepted. Getting it submitted should be considered as much a part of the work as the work itself. If you think it may be late, or you think you need an extension, do not ask us, simply use one of your exemptions.
  • PDF formats preferred, but our standard is, “Can we open the submission immediately without having to dig around, email you, etc.?”
  • 15 weeks in the semester.
  • We offer assignments in 13 of these weeks (no work due over holiday breaks!).
  • You must do exactly 10 weeks of work.
  • I.E. You need to use exactly 3 exemptions.

Some Things That Captured Our Attention

Dirt Batteries to Make Solar PV as Cheap (and reliable) as Natural Gas

Wakon Yosai: Or why Europe is all hot and bothered … “So the death rate from heat in Europe is almost twice the death rate from guns in America. If you think guns are an emergency in the U.S., you should think that heat in Europe is an even bigger emergency. Most of this death is preventable. The technology that prevents it is air conditioning”

Who Loves the Carbon Tax? Not the younger, poorer and less educated human beings that bear the disproportionate burden of it

Who Loves Nuclear? Not the ladies. “Nuclear power, which combines insignificant greenhouse gas emissions and air pollution with the reliability of fossil fuels, is another technology the progress movement strongly backs as a supplement to solar. There is a 30—point sex gap in support for increasing nuclear power generation in the US, and being male is one of the strongest predictors of support for nuclear in Denmark.”

Do Export Bans Protect Antiquities?

Damn … that’s a wee bit bigger than the Hoover Dam (actually, it is SIXTY times larger)

Temperature~economic growth: David Barker analyzes the Economic Inquiry article by Michael Kiley, who declined to respond to Barker’s critique of the Fed working paper version. Barker addresses what’s new and what he thinks is wrong. (Kiley is hereby invited to reply in a future issue of this journal.)  “Kiley’s results remain spurious, resulting from a combination of outlier observations, heteroskedasticity, and correlations of time with both GDP growth and temperature. Autocorrelation of growth and temperature exacerbate the spuriousness of his findings.”

Anger Drives Engagement … Sigh: “The results show that negative emotions increase support for protectionism, restrictive immigration policies, redistribution, and climate policies but do not reinforce populist attitudes. In contrast, positive emotions have little effect on policy preferences but reduce populist inclinations. Finally, distinguishing between fear and anger, we find that anger exerts a much stronger influence on citizens’ policy views, in line with its growing presence in the political rhetoric.”

Green Gas: Fracking reduced emissions per capita by 7.5% and accelerated move to renewables

Ban Drinking: Paper straws have more forever chemicals than plastic straws
