Some Things That Captured Our Attention

Your Neighbor is Not Your Enemy.

Small Shareholders “Free Ride” on Pollution Reduction: “First, small shareholders will systematically vote for a greener corporate profile. Second, firms with a smaller weighted median shareholder will pollute less. Third, countries with concentrated corporate wealth holdings and/or more individualized firm ownership pollute more.”

Superforecasters: Too pessimistic on AI, too optimistic on … green energy.

Intellectual Property Promotes Drug Development: We show that when intellectual property rights become unenforceable, research investment and commercialization nearly cease. In doing so, we test two claims central both to our model and the innovation literature more generally—that stronger

intellectual property protection does, in fact, induce investment, and that heterogeneity in the availability of these rights distorts investment. The welfare consequences of inadequate incentives in our empirical context are large. Our estimates suggest that 200-800 new uses for existing drugs would have been developed under counterfactual policies.

Flush Externalities Down the … THRONE

Fun Facts to Know and Tell: single family “sprawl” homes are (when you slice the data to make it look this way) more energy efficient than dense apartments. Sure to be shouted from every (apartment) rooftop, right?

Keepin’ Cool – Personal Ways to Adapt to a Heating Planet: “At the micro level, personal technological solutions are emerging rapidly. Wearable neck fans provide portable cooling, while phase-change materials integrated into clothing can absorb and release body heat to maintain comfortable body temperatures. Cooling vests used by outdoor workers can significantly reduce heat stress during physical labor.”
