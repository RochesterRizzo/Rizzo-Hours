The 84-year-old plant makes about 3% of what it did during peak production in 1944, according to Hacker’s research, and it has been beset by explosions and pollution violations. Your High Schools Suck, Episode 233928: “Education is widely recognized as the primary driver of human capital accumulation. However, policies designed to promote educational attainment often overlook students’ endogenous responses. This paper provides empirical evidence of the causal impact of relaxed academic standards on student effort and achievement. We find that academic leniency results in mechanical GPA increases, but significant reductions in effort among lower ability students based on lower attendance and lower numerical grades. These heterogeneous effects compound and exacerbate gaps in engagement and achievement throughout high school, ultimately worsening deficits in human capital accumulation as measured by ACT scores.” And then they come to college and wonder …

We Already Know What a World Without Work Looks Like? “Clara on Asterisk: We Already Know What A World Without Work Looks Like. The debate on what will happen if AI takes all the jobs usually swings between poles of “it will be great because we can focus on the finer things in life” and “it will be terrible because we’ll lose all meaning”. Clara brings her trademark careful historical scholarship and proposes a third option - groups ranging from hunter-gatherers to aristocrats have lived without productive work for thousands of years, and they spend their time in what could charitably be called “social grooming” and uncharitably “court intrigue” - pointless alliance-forming and backbiting in the pursuit of arbitrary status markers divorced from productive activity. “Aristocrats are basically chimpanzees. They’re highly social, cunning, at all times hyperaware of their strict internal dominance hierarchy, and constantly, obsessively tracking alliances, challenges, displays of prowess, and attempts to get ahead.” My only objection to this analogy is that some aristocrats were genuinely better at status games than others and probably had a lot of fun, whereas in the post-AI future the winners of status games will probably be those who check every move with a smarter-than-human AI prompted to help them optimize their status (and if everyone knows this to be true, it’s not clear what the ensuing status games actually look like)”

Maybe You Should Tax the Itch to Tax? We estimate that California's billionaires contribute $3.3–5.8 billion annually in state income taxes. The present value of permanently lost income tax collections from departures more than offsets the one-time wealth tax revenue, yielding a net present value of negative $38.9 billion. Across 100,000 simulated revenue and discount rate combinations, 85 percent of plausible outcomes produce a negative net present value of state revenues.

PHOTO OF THE WEEK:
“Allegedly, the riots gripping France are about school quality or income inequality, but anyone with eyes and ears knows that’s all nonsense.” City Hall in Limoges survived WW1 and then Hitler, but not the rioters.

A sad irony is that many people don’t realize that France is arguably the most socialist country in the entire world. Not in the “communist” sense of socialist, rather in the sense of democratic socialism—Bernie Sanders ideology. Sanders often cites Denmark, but his actual views are nothing at all like ultra-neoliberal Denmark. But they are similar to France. Lots of public services, lots of income redistribution, lots of regulation. Please name a country that fits that description better than France. I can’t think of one.

And yet somehow this near perfect example of democratic socialism seems to have produced such a dystopian society that angry young men burn down historic buildings like this one.

Of course I’m being sarcastic, France is still a nice place. But its economic model is clearly dysfunctional. The protesters want more government spending, but what France needs to do—and eventually will do—is cut back on government spending.

Coming soon to a theater near you. Some people think the voting age should be increased to 21, or 25.
