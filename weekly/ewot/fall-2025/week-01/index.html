Course timing and readings

We jump right into class materials on Day One. We will not spend class time going over the syllabus or doing introductions or anything of that sort. Our first few weeks will be spent trying to fan away the miasma of economics apprehension, and to help you understand our goals for the course. We are not here to go through a textbook and just check the boxes, we aim to give you a full-blooded introduction to the EWOT. Why dedicate tens of thousands of dollars in tuition if you are going to get here what you can get for free in simple format online (by the way there is still a good answer to that particular question, kudos to you for taking a stab at it)?

Begin working your way through the readings in Section One. There is no particular order to them, and as you will see that none of these is "required" in the traditional sense of the term. If you wish to only read a little bit, please focus on i, Pencil. The Engerman paper is a nice historical examination of what you are about to get yourselves into and the short Otteson Chapter is a very good attempt to define some terms that are ill-tossed around. Of course, there is never a wrong time to go through the materials in the Ur-Readings section.

Our lectures do not track the readings and vice-versa, but they are not there for no reason.

See “Readings, Videos and Reserves” for the class reading list. This will be the first of a zillion times you will hear me curse Blackboard – it is not very user friendly. It won’t easily let me create an html reading list, but I hacked it together a bit. There is a word doc you can download that has live links. On much of it, you need to be logged into our library and Rochester VPN/campus network to access the professional journals. Creative internet searching can probably find most of the material if you did want to navigate through our library or can’t get a link to work. Please read my comments on both the reading list and the syllabus regarding what I think of the readings and how you should engage with them.

Finally, USE CHAT GPT and its sister applications to take advantage of what is in the reading list. It is easy to find software now that you can feed one or more readings in and get summaries, comparisons, and more. With the application of AI, there is almost no excuse for not being engaged in some manner with our reading list. In fact, the Chat is a useful agent to help expand the list, and to have it sync up with materials you may be covering in other classes.

Assignments and due dates

And the treat you’ve all been waiting for … Week One Assignment
Your very first course assignments are posted on Blackboard under "Weekly Updates and Assignments 2025." Look for both a research and a writing (that sounds dreadful, how about "Rumination?") assignment.

For this week only, you are to choose EITHER the rumination or the research assignment. In all subsequent weeks we will have you complete both assignments.

The assignment is due the first Sunday night of the semester, August 31st, before 6:59pm. While I tend to write a lot myself, we do not expect you to. There are no limits or word specifications on the work, we ask that you spend the time early and often to engage with the questions and even chat about them with your peers.

You can submit your assignment on Blackboard under "Weekly Updates and Assignments 2025" then to "Week One Class Update and Assignment" and then to "Week One Rumination Assignment" if you choose the Rumination and “Week One Research Assignment" if you choose the Research.

Normally, you can expect your assignments to be posted two Fridays prior to their due dates. Hence, an assignment that is due on September 7th is likely to be posted on Friday, August 29th.

Assignment Planning for the Term, and Exemptions

  • Your week one assignment will NOT be counted in your course grade.

    • We are using it to give you a flavor for the kinds of weekly work you will be doing, to practice submitting your work, to get used to navigating blackboard, and to have the TAs provide you with a little feedback on your work before you dive deeply into the semester work.

    • If you choose not to submit week one homework, there is no penalty. That is a choice you make for yourself.

  • We will go live in Week two with two big assignments.

  • Planning for your exemptions, and what work to do?

    • Note there are 15 total weeks in the term.

    • We will assign work every week.

    • We won’t count week one in course grades.

    • Students are required to skips three weeks of the ruminations and research.

    • Thus, by the end of the term students have to do ELEVEN rumination assignments and ELEVEN research assignments, no more, no less.

    • Students do not have to take skips on BOTH parts in the same week.

    • If students have time troubles, or something comes up, use the exemptions then.

    • If students feel like their work is incomplete or they are going to hand it in (even one second) late, use the exemptions.

    • I recommend being smart about holding onto exemptions for when you are really busy later in the term. Note that if there is a week and you take an exemption, I may reproduce the skipped assignment for a future week.

    • Finally, it is possible that one or more weeks of homework is a “double” assignment. In those cases a skipped assignment would take up two exemptions.

    • If you choose an exemption, you do not need to do anything – leave your submission BLANK. We will enter a zero in that week of work. At the end of the term, if you did all of your work, you should have grades in 11 weeks and zeroes in three weeks. We only penalize students who have four or more weeks of zeros.

Exam information

Please make note of the Exam dates and times. The exams are held outside of class-time. Do not make travel arrangements that conflict with either of our two midterms or our final exam. Your first exam is on Friday, September 26th at 2pm. Yes, “it is on the test,” and be prepared to explain your thinking in clear, concise writing. Yes, we have a make-up slot for students with conflicts. And yes, we make arrangements for students traveling for sports or other school functions. No, we do not make arrangements if you are not on campus for non-UR reasons.

Recitation information

If you have not already done so, please register for a weekly recitation. There is more information about these on the syllabus and the "Recitations" link on Blackboard. These are not merely reviews of what we do in class. You can see which recitations have openings in it by looking at the course registration information from the registrar. The recitations are more enjoyable than the classes in my view, and we have worked hard to find space around campus and times during the week to accommodate ~230 students and 14 Teaching Assistants. This was no easy task. The sections are each capped at 24, and you may not attend recitations other than the one you registered for. We have scheduled several evening recitations - so while those may not be the most convenient for you, we are hoping that there are enough times scheduled so as to make it possible for every student to attend a recitation without conflict. Please do not send us long explanations for why you MUST MUST MUST get into a particular recitation. We understand that you are busy and have many other things going on. But this is true for 230 classmates as well. We do our best to accommodate you with the broad offerings, so please find one that you can attend, and I hope you take the chance to get to engage with the TAs and your peers in those sessions.
