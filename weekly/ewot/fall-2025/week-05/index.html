Course timing and readings

We spend a few weeks or more on our Economic Evolution – I try to break it up into two pieces, first the historical record and second a consideration of the ideas that have contributed to it. You can read through BOTH Sections B and C of the reading list at your leisure, we are soon headed into the “How Did This All Happen” part of the material.

You should NOT be memorizing the data we put up, that is not the purpose of our exploration.

Assignments and due dates

You have your AI Superforecaster research assignment, and your “F&ck the Poor” rumination assignment this Sunday the 21st.

You DO also have homework due for exam week, but it is ONE single assignment that will count for both your research and rumination assignment. We will be exploring protest via the medium of music, I very much enjoyed thinking about and writing that one too, I hope you try to work on it.

Exam information

Exam: Please see the Blackboard Website and the Course Syllabus for additional information

  • The exam will be given in HOYT Auditorium from 2:00pm to 5:00pm on Friday the 26th.

  • If you have special accommodations from ODR, you will work directly with them on the timing and location of the exam.

  • If you have a class conflict, and have completed the Google Doc that is on Blackboard, the make-up exam will be on Friday morning, September 26th, from 7:30am to 10:15am in Harkness 115. Students who have not filled out the Google Doc will not be admitted to the room.

  • We DO HAVE CLASSES on the day of the exam.

  • The exam will be open note, open book, open Google, open ChatGPT, etc. You may use any resource you wish, including lectures that you recorded from the class. The only restriction is that you may NOT communicate with other people about the exam, it must be done completely “on your own.”

NOTE: We will be checking to see if you are on emails, social media, text or any other app that enables you to communicate with others. You may not communicate with others. If you are suspected, we will refer your case directly to the Academic Honesty Committee.

  • Make sure you have your Student ID on you, you will not be permitted to turn the exam in without it.

  • Make sure you know the name of your recitation TA, as you need to include this information on the exam, and you will lose two points for not including this information on your exam.

The exam materials will cover everything I do through the final seconds of class this upcoming Wednesday, September 24th. All exam policies can be found on Blackboard under the exam section. It is your responsibility to have read and understood them. Be well rested as the exam will require considerable reading, and require you to write out your answers. Yes, “it is on the test,” and be prepared to explain your thinking in clear, concise writing. I will be posting some example questions to blackboard as I begin my preparations to write it. We will not be running a formal review session for the exam, there are non-trivial reasons, including pedagogical ones, for why we have made that decision.

The Tortures (Most Work is Not “Real” Work):

Only 39% of the workday is spent on core job duties. The rest is emails, meetings, and admin.

Knowledge workers spend 57–60% of their time coordinating and communicating.

Developers write new code just 10–16% of their week. Most of their time is debugging, planning, or searching for context.

Meetings alone consume 14.8 hours per week, about 37% of working hours, costing nearly $30,000 per employee each year.

No wonder org charts look the way they do. They’re pyramids: leaders at the top, doers at the bottom, and a thick middle band of human translators carrying information up and down.”

The Polarization and Profit Death Loop: “Firms’ profits are higher when they cater to workers with extreme preferences. In a competitive assignment equilibrium, firms become polarized and not only reflect but also amplify the polarized preferences of the general population. More polarized sectors exhibit higher profits, lower average wages, and a reduced labor share of value added. Sustainable investing amplifies firm polarization.”
