WEEKLY REMINDERS/ANNOUNCEMENTS – EWOT, Week 1

Read the Syllabus
The syllabus and all class materials are on the Blackboard website. Please read the syllabus and related course descriptions carefully. All policies related to grades, assignments, etc. are on the syllabus. Please think twice and three-times before sending admin-related emails that are covered by the syllabus. For example, “What date and time is our first midterm?” Ask a classmate first. Ask a TA second. Ask the Head TA third.

Or DON’T Read the Syllabus: https://chatgpt.com/g/g-6a763182a9d08191b73bba0904ed9144-econ-108-syllabus-what-do-you-need-to-know

Explore Blackboard
The entire site is clunky, but it is the official communication source, and all class materials we wish to share will be in repository there. If you hear something about the course from someone, and you cannot find it on our ChatBot or Blackboard, assume it is not the case.

Topics This Week
We jump right into class materials on Day One. We will not spend class time going over the syllabus or doing introductions or anything of that sort. Our first few weeks will be spent trying to fan away the miasma of economics apprehension, and to help you understand our goals for the course. We are not here to go through a textbook and just check the boxes, we aim to give you a full-blooded introduction to the EWOT.

Readings This Week
Begin working your way through the readings in Section One. There is no particular order to them, and as you will see that none of these is “required” in the traditional sense of the term. If you wish to only read a little bit, please focus on i, Pencil. The Engerman paper is a nice historical examination of what you are about to get yourselves into and the short Otteson Chapter is a very good attempt to define some terms that are ill-tossed around. Of course, there is never a wrong time to go through the materials in the Ur-Readings section.

Or Don’t Read Anything At All - the UNReading List: https://chatgpt.com/g/g-6a6b783f83a881919379581ca86905d8-the-ewot-unreading-list

Do You Need to Be a Billionaire to Live Your Dreams? “I am reminded of a scene in the movie Wall Street where Bud, the protagonist, is working in a high-paying finance job he hates so that he can make a million dollars, retire and ride a motorcycle across China. The inside joke is that you don’t need to work on Wall Street, or to make a million dollars, to buy a motorcycle to cross China. You could easily do that whole trip for $2,000 dollars, saved from a short summer stint working at Costco … The bottleneck for most dreams is not financial. It’s a lack of confidence, willingness to take a risk, and face failure, or a lack of imagination of what is possible with current resources. If you want to write a novel you don’t need capital — you need discipline and 1,000 words a day. If you want to start a consultancy you need expertise and a first client, not a war chest. A lack of money is a convenient thing to blame because it feels concrete, but it’s rarely the actual blocker for achievements.”
